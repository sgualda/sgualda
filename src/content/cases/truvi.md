I am a product designer at truvi, a startup in Barcelona. Among other things I work on the dashboard.

This page is deliberately short. It is where I work right now, so what I can write about is how I work rather than what we are building — the specifics belong to the company, not to my portfolio. What follows is the part that is mine: what the job is teaching me.

## Working with people who are not designers

Most of my day is not design work. It is conversation with engineers, product managers and stakeholders, all of whom are right about different things and none of whom are looking at the same problem I am.

I used to treat that as friction to get through. **It is closer to the actual job.** An engineer telling me something is expensive is not an obstacle to the design, it is information about the design that I did not have. The version I would have shipped without that conversation would have been worse and slower.

## Showing work earlier than is comfortable

The change I have made that mattered most is showing things before they are ready.

My instinct is to disappear, work it out, and come back with something coherent. It feels more professional. It is also how you spend two weeks going in a direction that one five-minute conversation would have corrected.

So now I show the ugly version. **It is less comfortable and it is faster**, and I still have to make myself do it every time — the pull towards presenting a finished thing does not go away, it just gets easier to ignore.

## Feedback is not a performance review

Early on I took feedback on the work as feedback on me. Everyone says not to. Knowing that and feeling it are different things.

What helped was a small reframe: the people giving me notes have spent longer with this product than I have. When somebody pushes back, they usually know something I do not. My job in that moment is to find out what it is, not to defend what I made.

## Still learning

I am aware that everything above sounds like the kind of thing people write when they have figured it out. I have not. I still over-polish, I still get attached to solutions, and I still occasionally build the elaborate version when the simple one would have done.

The difference is that now I notice sooner, which is most of what improving actually looks like.
