ecoco started on a road trip from Barcelona to Bordeaux. Two friends — a developer and a designer — driving through France, talking about nothing in particular, until somebody asked why grocery shopping is still such a mess. Lists get lost. Things get forgotten. The same basket costs different money in three shops on the same street.

It is a small problem. It is also everybody's problem, which is the combination worth paying attention to.

## What we actually did first

No business plan. No investors. No deadline. What we had was curiosity and a want to build something from nothing.

I did what I would do for any client: I went and talked to people. Families doing a weekly shop, students splitting a flat, my own parents. The patterns showed up quickly and they were not the ones I expected. Nobody asked for a better list. They asked for **two things: not forgetting, and knowing whether they were being overcharged.**

Paper sketches became prototypes. Prototypes became things people could hold. And an app slowly took a shape neither of us had drawn at the start.

![Early ecoco screens: list building and the price comparison view](../../assets/cases/ecoco2.jpg)

## The unromantic part

The tidy version of this story would end there. The truth is that it was messy, and I would rather write down the messy version.

We were two people with jobs, studies and lives. We designed flows nobody understood. I overcomplicated screens because they looked better that way, and then watched somebody fail to use them in front of me, which is a specific kind of quiet.

**We badly underestimated how long testing takes.** Not the sessions — those are quick. The part where you have to sit with what you heard and admit that the thing you spent three weekends on is not the thing.

Progress was slow. Sometimes painfully slow. But every failed prototype and every late call made it a bit less wrong.

## Where it is now

ecoco is in private beta on Android, under review by Google Play. You can build and manage lists, compare supermarket prices, and reuse a list week after week — that last one turned out to matter far more than we thought, because a weekly shop is mostly the same shop.

![The current build, running on Android](../../assets/cases/ecoco3.jpg)

## Three things it taught me

**Constraints fuel creativity.** Two people and no money is a filter. You cannot build the elaborate version, so you have to find out which small version is the one that matters. Most of what we cut has never come back.

**Iteration beats perfection.** The prototypes I was proudest of are not the ones that survived. The ones that survived were rougher and answered a question.

**Humility is key**, and it is the one I keep having to relearn. I am a product designer, which means my instincts are trained — and being trained is exactly what makes it easy to trust them past the point where you should have gone and asked somebody.

## What is next

More users, a better experience, and continuing to learn as it grows. It is not a company yet. It might not become one. It has already been worth it.
