## The one thing that decides this

Your competition is not other product designers. Nobody searches for a product
designer by name.

They search for **the problem they have at 11pm**, and your six free checks are
already written for exactly those moments. That is unusual and it is the whole
opportunity: a portfolio competes with thousands of portfolios, a diagnostic
tool competes with blog posts that do not answer the question.

So the strategy is not "rank for product designer Barcelona". It is: **own the
queries people type when a product is not working**, and let the site do the
introduction.

**"Why is nobody using my product" is the single best asset on this site.** It is
a real query, typed in desperation, with no satisfying answer currently ranking —
most results are generic growth-marketing listicles. You have a five-way
diagnostic written by somebody who got it wrong twice.

**Two of the six have a naming problem.** *"Reversible vs irreversible decisions"*
and *"why does your team keep redoing the same work"* are how **you** describe
those problems, not how anybody searches for them. Likely real phrasings:

- *"how long should i spend on a decision"* · *"one way vs two way door decisions"*
- *"team keeps revisiting the same decisions"* · *"why do we keep having the same argument at work"*

Worth testing as H1 variants once there is Search Console data to compare against.

## The gaps worth filling

Ranked by ratio of demand to effort.

**1 · The five reasons, as an essay.**
The check tells you which of five you are in, but there is no page that simply
explains the five. That page targets *"why is nobody using my product"*
informationally, where the check targets it diagnostically. Together they cover
both intents and cross-link.

**2 · One essay per map stage.**
Five pages, each targeting a decision-intent query, each already outlined by
the stage page it belongs to. The stage pages are short by design; the essays
are where the depth goes.

**3 · A glossary.**
`/glossary/` with 15–20 terms — *workaround*, *repeat use*, *reversible
decision*, *polite no*. Defined terms are what a language model quotes when
asked what something means. Ticket `#Q-074`.

**4 · The two rewrites already identified.**
`ux-certification-worth-it` — a real question with a personal answer, 236 words
long. `learning-to-let-go-an-idea` — the most on-brand subject you have, 242
words. Both unpublished, both worth 900–1,200 words.

**5 · Comparison pages.**
Comparisons rank well because the intent is unambiguous and the competition is
thin. *"Product discovery vs user research"*, *"MVP vs prototype vs pilot"*,
*"design critique vs design review"*.

## What not to chase

**"Product designer Barcelona".** Low volume, and whoever searches it is
recruiting rather than buying. Your LinkedIn already handles that.

**"UX design".** Enormous, informational, and the traffic converts into nothing
for a consultant.

**Anything about Figma.** You have a good essay arguing design is not about
Figma. Do not build a cluster around a tool you are arguing against.

## How to verify any of this

Everything above is reasoning, not data. To turn it into data:

1. **Verify Search Console by DNS now**, before the migration. It starts
   collecting on the current WordPress and gives you a baseline.
2. After four weeks, open **Performance → Queries**. That is a free list of
   what you *already* rank for, which is better than any estimate.
3. Look for queries with impressions but a poor position — those are pages that
   nearly rank, and they are the cheapest wins available.
4. Only then consider a paid tool. Search Console will probably tell you what
   you need.

**Update this file when the data arrives.** An SEO plan built on estimates and
never revisited is worse than no plan, because it looks like a decision.
